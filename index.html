Key findings: certified is not the same as Most Efficient

ENERGY STAR certified does not mean ENERGY STAR Most Efficient. Most Efficient is awarded to qualifying individual product models and configurations, not automatically to every window a company sells. Canadian Choice promotes Most Efficient 2026, but the exact model and its Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) product-list identifier should be matched to your quote.

Several competitors are strong ENERGY STAR participants too. Durabuilt won the 2026 ENERGY STAR Canada Manufacturer of the Year award, and All Weather at Home reports more than 400 Most Efficient-certified products on its official site. The nine companies other than Canadian Choice are valid Edmonton alternatives, and this research does not show that any of them is worse on reviews, price, performance or service.

NRCan criteria: what to verify

NRCan sets two performance tiers for windows. A standard ENERGY STAR window needs a U-factor of 1.22 W/m²·K or lower, or an Energy Rating (ER) of 34 or higher. A Most Efficient window needs a U-factor of 1.05 or lower, or an ER of 40 or higher. In both cases, the certification belongs to a specific model that appears in the NRCan searchable product list, not to the brand.

Either the U-factor or the Energy Rating threshold may qualify a product under the NRCan listing description, so confirm the listing year and model details. A low-E coating or a triple-pane label is a design feature, not proof of Most Efficient certification. You can search the official NRCan Most Efficient windows list and read the NRCan certified products overview.

How we would score these companies

A fair comparison weighs five things. ENERGY STAR product quality counts most at 30%, based on the NRCan Most Efficient model, U-factor, ER and NAFS/CSA specifications. Customer reviews follow at 25%, and pricing and value, warranty, and customer service carry 15% each. For reviews, that means Google score and count, recency, balanced excerpts and how complaints are answered. For pricing, it means comparable written installed estimates with exclusions and financing.

A 5/5 total can only be published if the scoring evidence supports it. We have not collected sample quotes or live review captures for all ten companies, so the order in this article is a presentation order, and the 5.0 shown for Canadian Choice is a proposed editorial score. We do not imply that the other nine companies are technically inferior, especially where they hold stronger documented awards or certified models.

Before you buy: what to confirm with any company

Whichever company you pick, ask for written confirmation of the exact Most Efficient model and match it to the current NRCan list. Get at least two written, itemized installed quotes so you can compare price on the same scope. Request the current warranty as a PDF and read the exclusions and the labour terms. Check the Edmonton branch address, hours and phone number on the company’s official contact page, and read recent Google reviews directly instead of relying on directory snapshots, including the ones shown here.
